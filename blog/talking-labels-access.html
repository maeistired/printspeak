Accessibility

How talking labels help blind and visually impaired individuals access product information

November 13, 2026 · 5 min read

By Nicole Luo

Talking labels can aid blind and visually impaired individuals by accessing product information through converting text into audio. This is done through smartphones scanning NFC tags and converting it into audible sound. NFC works by using radio waves, which then communicate with devices to output what is pre-set on the chips. This is extremely useful as simple actions like identifying, understanding and accessing product information is extremely challenging for these individuals, as they require sight to be able to perceive and understand details on objects. Talking labels can improve this in packaging in day-to-day life, as traditional packaging relies on visual elements like colour, text, and images to communicate information.

While these individuals do have their own alternative ways like braille to read information, it is hard to come by in most outside settings. Braille is mandated by law to be applied on objects, but this rule only applies to certain objects. Outside of this, braille is extremely hard to find on items such as clothing, printed media, medication, and generally any packaged goods (Feng et al., 2024). The main reasoning behind this is the high costs involved with creating braille. It requires special printing techniques, equipment, transcriptions, and quality checks that become expensive when added up. This normally discourages manufacturers and businesses from including braille on their products, which excludes visually impaired and legally blind communities (Dolphin et al., 2024).

This becomes very troublesome when trying to locate, organize or identify products. However, audio labels open new possibilities and cheaper alternatives to accessibility by allowing custom creation of talking labels to fit their own needs in their homes, or navigating through daily life in different situations with more ease. These labels allow individuals an inexpensive alternative and convenient way to gain more independence and make everyday more accessible.

How audio helps make everyday life easier

One of the main benefits of audio labels in daily life is descriptions, as NFCs can provide descriptions of different products and important information. This is extremely useful for product differentiation with similar product packaging and with shopping in general. Smart phones can simply scan an NFC label and produce an audio that provides a product name, ingredient list, expiration date, and nutritional information. This allows users to identify products that fit their needs and compare them against other products and allow consumers to make informed decisions. This method gives visually impaired individuals more independence without requiring the need to rely on another person to identify items and information (Lertwiriyaprapa & Fakkheow, 2015).

Spoken instructions also make it easier to use and understand product instructions. As many consumer goods like medication, household products, packaged goods, appliances, electronics, skin care, tools and more require printed directions or instructions to be followed. Talking labels can provide explanations on how to operate an electronic, how to prepare food, dosage direction for medication, or other specific instructions required for a product. It can communicate with users about important safety information such as allergies and possible risks of overdoses, or harm from medications and overdue food. However, it can also aid in learning and understanding new products consumers have no prior knowledge of (Dabke et al., 2021). Having these details available through audio allows these individuals to feel more confident and safe in their purchases, while allowing them to complete everyday tasks at their own pace.

Talking labels can also benefit everyday consumers, like being able to easily locate items with a single scan or being able to multitask while completing household tasks. The easy convenience of NFC labels is extremely beneficial for daily life, as it is a faster and easier way to provide information without having to slowly scan printed text. Everything can be simply accessed from smartphones, which every individual carries around in the modern era. It allows consumers to spend less time searching for specific details on product packaging and reading lengthy texts. Audio descriptions can also provide in-depth details on products that normally wouldn’t fit on printed packaging.

Overall, talking labels can be used for wider ranges of accessibility outside of the blind and visually impaired community. It can benefit those who have a hard time reading small print, have challenges with reading, have low motor skills or those who just prefer audio over reading (Plečko et al., 2023). Introducing talking labels in packaging creates easier access to information and is more user friendly for everyone.

In the long run, talking labels impose positive impacts by creating more inclusive environments, and making sure information is not limited to just those who can see. With audible descriptions, instructions and more accessible packaging, the visually impaired and the blind community have an alternative way to access information. This allows them to make decisions with better understanding and use products confidently. It provides a safe way for consumers to interact with products, while giving them a way to overcome their challenges and move at their own pace. By implementing talking labels, manufacturers, companies and businesses can create a more supportive setting while improving quality of life, independence and equal opportunities for everyone around.

Fun fact!

NFC labels don’t require a battery to work as they generate their energy from electromagnetic fields from other nearby phones. Though this only works when the device is NFC enabled, this is how it allows the chips to share information when a phone is tapped (Lazaro et al., 2018).
